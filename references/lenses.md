# The five lenses

One search angle never finds a training market. Five researchers, each blind to the others, cover it; the verifier merges them. Build the five prompts for the country from `country-playbooks.md` (seeds and search phrases), then run them in parallel.

Every lens prompt gets the same BRIEF first (who the user is, the offer, the exclusions, the holds, the date, "open the source yourself and give the URL you opened, mark uncertain items, no em dashes") and the same return contract: 8 to 15 candidates, each with name, website, type, one line on what they do, evidence they deliver corporate training, co-financing access (none / listed provider of scheme X / fund beneficiary / EDIH partner), English capability if visible, why it fits the user's courses, contact (general email, phone, named person and role if public), source URL, a one-line pitch angle, confidence (verified / likely / uncertain).

| Lens | What it finds | How to adapt it to a country |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Follow the money | The providers that public or sector training money already pays. Beneficiary associations, accredited or procured provider lists, fund catalogues, voucher-eligible providers, e-procurement notices for "training". These have budget and clients today. | Start from the playbook's funding instruments and existing lists. Name the registers and the beneficiary organisations explicitly in the prompt, and ask for the PROVIDERS they list, not the scheme itself. |
| 2. Commercial IT and digital trainers | B2B IT, digital and AI training companies with a public catalogue: Microsoft, Google and AWS training partners, coding schools with corporate arms, large adult-education providers, e-learning producers. | Seed with 5 to 10 names from the playbook plus "authorised training partner <vendor> <country>". Ask for catalogue topics (AI, Copilot, Workspace, automation) and delivery language. |
| 3. Schools, chambers, hubs | Business schools' executive and continuing education, universities' lifelong-learning units, chambers of commerce and employer bodies with training arms, EDIH-type digital hubs and their partners, regional development agencies that run company programmes. | Seed with the exec-ed units and the chambers; ask for the programme director or corporate-training manager as the contact. Flag any organisation linked to a relationship the user already has. |
| 4. AI-specific players | Companies that already sell AI training or consulting to companies, AI associations, AI centres, tech companies with academies, trainer collectives. Partners for an English or Google-side bench, otherwise competitors. | Search in the local language for "AI courses for companies", "AI training for employees", "ChatGPT course companies <city>". Ask whether the user can be a partner lecturer rather than a competitor. |
| 5. Adjacent trainers | Sales, leadership, HR, marketing and communication training firms with no AI module yet, coaching federations, marketing agencies that run paid courses, seminar and conference organisers. Lowest competitor risk; fastest replies in Latvia. | Seed with the country's known sales and leadership brands (Mercuri, FranklinCovey, Dale Carnegie franchises, local leaders) and the HR and marketing associations' academies. Search phrases: "sales training for companies", "leadership training <city>", "marketing courses for companies". |

Rules that keep the sweep honest:
- Local language first, English second. In Latvia half the market only appeared for Latvian queries.
- A candidate without a URL the researcher opened is "uncertain" and the verifier treats it as a lead to check, not a fact.
- The exclusions (already contacted) and holds (linked to someone else's relationship) are stated in the BRIEF so researchers do not spend budget on them, but holds are still returned and marked, because the user decides on them later.
- Ask each lens for 8 to 15; five lenses give 40 to 70 raw rows and the verifier lands at 20 to 50.
